the serial against the code

the speaker in the story is invented. the tape is not, the numbers are not, and this page says day by day which is which.
the short version. a serial runs on @ventry089 about six agents saving a dying language from its last speaker. that man does not exist. the desk does: it runs on 1.2 hours of Torwali and 27 minutes of Piedmontese, both recorded by Wikitongues from real speakers, and every number below opens the second of audio it came from.

where the story goes past what a program can do, this table says so instead of quietly agreeing. three of the days need a human being in a room, and no amount of code closes that.

HALF OF IT RUNS

the one number that decides whether any of this counts

day 7 is the load-bearing one: a machine producing words nobody ever recorded. that is easy to fake - stack phones together and call the pile a language. so the desk runs a control on every pass: 1,110 random chains of exactly the same lengths, phones drawn by how common they are on this very tape.

0 of them land anywhere in 1,164 utterances. the derived forms hit 5 times. that gap is the difference between grammar and noise, and the line prints on every run - including the runs where it would kill the result.

on the Piedmontese tape it does kill it: the gate signed one rule out of 18, an analogy needs two, so DERIVE returns nothing at all and says why. 27 minutes is not enough tape. that is the desk working, not the language being empty.

what the desk will never do

judge whether a rule is a joke. hear that a word belonged to somebody's mother. decide that a pattern is dead. those need the speaker, and the gate exists exactly because the desk cannot do them. the serial is allowed to imagine that room. this repo is not.
