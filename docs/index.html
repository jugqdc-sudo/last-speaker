what this page proves, and what it does not

you can verify this yourself

  • the tape is real and so are the positions. click any timestamp and the recording plays right here, at that second. cmd+click opens the full tape on the source.
  • the chains really do repeat. a "word" here means an exact sequence of sounds that came back 14, 11, 9 times across 1,164 utterances. hear them and count.
  • the desk refuses more than it accepts. 41 of 60 rules were thrown out, each with the reason, listed below.
  • it is not pattern noise. 1,110 random chains of the same lengths were run through the same grammar: 0 of them land anywhere on this tape.
  • the code is open. same pipeline, your own recording, one command.

what it is not

  • nothing here is a translation. no dictionary was used and the desk does not know what any of it means. it never guesses a meaning.
  • "word" and "rule" are mechanical terms. a word is a repeating chain of sounds, a rule is a chain that attaches to many different stems. a linguist would call this a starting point, not a description of the language.
  • no native speaker has confirmed any of it. that is the whole problem of case 01: there is nobody left to ask.
  • the serial on the account is dramatised. the desk, the tape and every number here are real. the story around them is a story.

what this is not

no dictionary of the language was used and nothing here is a translation. a "word" is a chain of phones that repeats, a "rule" is a chain that keeps attaching to different neighbours, a "fight" is two of them one phone apart. the register detector hears pitch and tempo, not meaning. a linguist would call this a starting point. that is what it is, and every line carries the second of tape it came from.
